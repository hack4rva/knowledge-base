**Q: "What would co-hosting the reception actually involve?"**

A: "Minimal work, maximum impact. We're handling all logistics (Common House, invitations, materials). You'd be listed as co-host, have 3-5 minutes to speak about VR's vision for Richmond, and get introduced to all attendees. Think of it as high-value networking with Richmond's most influential leaders - Mayor, corporate CEOs, tech leaders - with you positioned as a key convener."

**Q: "Are you asking for money?"**

A: "Not for the November reception - Common House is covering that. For the March hackathon, we're seeking sponsors, but that's a separate conversation. If you love what you see at the reception and want to sponsor the main event, we'd be thrilled. But right now, we're just asking you to lend your name and presence as a community convener."

**Q: "Why Venture Richmond vs. other downtown organizations?"**

A: "You're THE downtown organization. This hackathon is about Richmond's future - economic vitality, innovation, problem-solving. VR's involvement signals to the business community that this is serious and strategic, not just a tech hobby event. Plus, several challenges directly support VR's mission - small business support, downtown activation, economic development."

**Q: "What's the time commitment?"**

A: "For November: Attend the 2-hour reception, speak for 3-5 minutes. That's it. For the March hackathon: We'd love your advisory input (a few conversations), potential speaking role at awards ceremony, and helping us connect to downtown businesses. All flexible based on your capacity."

**Q: "Who else is involved?"**

A: "Mayor's Office (Danny Avula is keynote), CoStar Group (hosting March event + executive involvement), RVA Tech (community partner), VCU (student participation), and we're in conversations with CarMax, Capital One, and Dominion. You'd be joining Richmond's civic and tech leadership coalition."

**Q: "What if the hackathon doesn't produce results?"**

A: "That's why we've built an implementation pathway. City departments are identifying challenges they actually want solved. We're requiring 'Civic Open Source' licensing so the City can adopt solutions. We're providing 6-month post-event mentorship and micro-grants. We're not leaving this to chance - we're engineering for implementation from day one."
