# Sponsor Perks & Engagement

**Based on MLH Best Practices for Sponsor Involvement**

Hackathons can find unique and fascinating ways of involving sponsors in different aspects of their event to benefit everyone involved. The range of perks you can offer your sponsors is limitless.

---

## 🏆 Running a Sponsored Prize

A sponsored prize track or category can significantly boost interest in your hackathon for the sponsors as well as the participants.

**For sponsors:** Could result in new people contributing in unique ways towards their product

**For hackers:** Additional prize for them to aim for during the hackathon!

### MLH Best Practice

**Match the value of your sponsors' prizes with the ones your team is providing** to avoid overshadowing other prize categories.

### Richmond Civic Hackathon Prize Structure

**Our Base Prizes:**
- Grand Prize (Mayor's Choice): $3,000
- 2nd Place: $2,000
- 3rd Place: $1,000

**Sponsored Prize Recommendations:**
- **Gold Tier Sponsors ($7,500-9,000)**: $2,000 prize category
- **Silver Tier Sponsors ($4,500-6,000)**: $1,000 prize category
- **Bronze Tier Sponsors ($2,000-3,000)**: $500 prize category

**Example Sponsored Prizes:**

1. **CoStar**: "PropTech for Public Good Award" ($2,000)
   - Best use of geospatial data or property information for civic benefit

2. **Dominion Energy**: "Sustainable Innovation Award" ($1,000)
   - Best solution addressing climate resilience or energy efficiency

3. **CarMax/Capital One**: "Best Student Team Award" ($1,000)
   - Recognize outstanding work from university students

4. **Technical Sponsor**: "Best Use of [Their API/Platform]" ($500-1,000)
   - Encourages use of their specific technology

## 🤝 Sponsor Interactions

### Representatives On-Site

**If your sponsors are running a prize track, request that they bring a representative who is familiar with their challenge.**

**Benefits:**
- Hackers can ask questions
- Get feedback during development
- Talk about the track in detail
- Learn about the company/technology

**This is the best way to guarantee meaningful interactions between hackers and sponsors**, and often leads to better project quality and sponsor satisfaction.

### Active Mentorship

**Encourage sponsors to send someone to actively mentor and start conversations with hackers.**

**Mentor responsibilities:**
- Circulate among teams
- Answer technical questions
- Provide feedback on approaches
- Share industry expertise
- Be approachable and friendly

### Sponsor Communication Channels

**For in-person events with Discord/Slack:**

Create **sponsor-specific channels** where they can:
- Introduce themselves to all participants
- Answer questions about their challenge/API
- Share resources and documentation
- Respond to questions in real-time
- Be accessible throughout event

**Richmond channels to create:**
- #general-announcements
- #sponsor-costar
- #sponsor-[company-name]
- #ask-mentors
- #technical-support

## 📚 Workshops

**Great way of keeping your hackers engaged and increasing the presence of your sponsors.**

### Timing Best Practices

**✅ DO schedule workshops:**
- **Friday early afternoon** (1-4 PM) - Introductory content
- **Friday evening** (7-9 PM) - Technical deep-dives
- **Saturday morning-afternoon** - Advanced topics

**❌ DON'T schedule workshops:**
- **Sunday** - Hackers will be exhausted (exceptions: "Pitching Your Project" or "Submitting Your Project")
- **Late night** - Low attendance
- **During meals** - People want to eat and socialize

### Workshop Timing Strategy

**Schedule introductory workshops early on** to give hackers enough time to experiment with what they learn during the hackathon.

### Workshop Examples

Based on MLH guide, here are workshop ideas for Richmond sponsors:

#### Technical Workshops

1. **Intro to [Sponsor's API/Platform]**
   - Example: "Building Civic Apps with AWS"
   - Example: "Intro to GitHub Copilot for Rapid Prototyping"

2. **Technical Skills**
   - "Intro to Python for Data Analysis"
   - "Building Web Apps with React"
   - "Intro to AI/ML for Civic Applications"
   - "Working with Geospatial Data"

#### Career Development Workshops

3. **Resume Building / Cracking the Technical Interview**
   - Perfect for recruiting-focused sponsors (CarMax, Capital One)
   - High attendance from students
   - Valuable beyond the hackathon

4. **Day in the Life of a Software Developer**
   - Company culture showcase
   - Real employee stories
   - Q&A session

#### Civic-Specific Workshops

5. **Working with Open Data**
   - How to access Richmond's open data
   - Data cleaning and analysis basics
   - Building dashboards from public datasets

6. **Designing for Citizens**
   - UX principles for civic applications
   - Accessibility requirements
   - User research for public services

## 🎮 Fun and Games

### Sponsor vs Hacker vs Organizer Challenges

**As you're hosting games and competitions throughout the event, mix it up with sponsor participation!**

**Benefits:**
- Breaks down barriers between sponsors and hackers
- Creates memorable interactions
- Shows sponsors as "real people"
- Fun photo opportunities
- Increases sponsor satisfaction

### Mini-Event Ideas with Sponsor Involvement

**For sponsors not interested in technical workshops**, see if they're interested in hosting a mini-event or game:

- Cup stacking competition
- Ping pong tournament
- Trivia contest (tech, Richmond, civic knowledge)
- Puzzle challenges
- Scavenger hunt
- Video game tournament

### Encourage Participation

**Some sponsors don't think they are allowed to participate in the fun mini-events.**

**Solution:** Have one of your organizers (potentially your sponsorship lead) go around before mini-events to get the sponsors to come take a break and hang with hackers!

**Richmond timing for mini-events:**
- Late afternoon Friday (4-5 PM) - Break between workshops
- Evening Friday (9-10 PM) - Wind-down before closing

## 💼 Recruiting Opportunities

**One perk many sponsors find valuable is strengthening their talent pipeline** by talking to hackers.

### Recruiting-Focused Perks

Depending on the time of year, sponsors may be interested in various opportunities:

#### 1. Resume Critiques

**Format:** Office hours where recruiters help hackers edit and perfect their resumes

**Logistics:**
- Dedicated quiet space
- 15-20 minute slots
- Sign-up sheet or first-come-first-served
- Friday afternoon or Saturday

**Richmond implementation:**
- Private room at CoStar HQ
- CarMax/Capital One recruiters
- 2-3 hour windows

#### 2. 1:1 Recruiter/Sponsorship Fair

**Format:** Dedicated space where hackers can chat with sponsors/recruiters about anything

**Topics:**
- Resumes
- Job expectations
- Company culture
- Open positions
- Career paths

**Logistics:**
- Booth/table space in common area
- Throughout the event
- Casual, drop-in style
- Name tags for recruiters

**Richmond implementation:**
- Friday afternoon (2-5 PM) and evening (7-9 PM)
- Sponsor tables in lobby or common space
- Relaxed, coffee-chat atmosphere

#### 3. On-Site Interviews

**Format:** If you're providing resumes in advance, recruiters may be interested in interviewing hackers during the event

**Logistics:**
- **Use dedicated office-style space** to respect privacy of interview process
- Pre-scheduled time slots
- Resume collection during registration
- Quiet, professional environment

**Richmond implementation:**
- Private conference rooms at CoStar
- Friday or Saturday slots
- Optional for participants
- Advance sign-up required

⚠️ **Important:** On-site interviews should be optional and not interfere with hacking time

## 🎁 Additional Sponsor Perks

### Swag Distribution

**Sponsors can distribute their own swag:**
- T-shirts, stickers, pens, etc.
- At their booth/table
- During workshops
- In welcome bags (if arranged in advance)

**Richmond logistics:**
- Coordinate swag quantities in advance
- Designate distribution space
- Include in sponsor welcome packet

### Branded Meals/Snacks

**Higher-tier sponsors can brand specific meals:**

Example: "Dinner Friday night powered by Dominion Energy"

**Benefits:**
- Prominent mention during meal time
- Signage at food area
- Announcements to all participants
- Social media tagging

### Social Media Features

**Sponsor-specific social media content:**

- Pre-event: "We're excited to partner with [Company]!"
- During event: Workshop/activity highlights
- Post-event: Thank you posts with photos

**Frequency by tier:**
- Diamond: 5-7 posts
- Gold: 3-4 posts
- Silver: 2-3 posts
- Bronze: 1-2 posts

### VIP Networking Access

**Higher-tier sponsors get exclusive access to:**

- Mayor and City leadership
- CoStar executives
- Other sponsor representatives
- Judges and mentors
- Community leaders

**Format:** Friday evening VIP reception (6:30-7:30 PM)

## 📞 Maintaining Company Relations

### During the Event

**Keep sponsors informed:**
- Daily email updates
- Real-time photos and updates
- Check in with representatives
- Address any issues immediately
- Make them feel valued

**Assign a sponsor liaison:**
- One organizer responsible for sponsor happiness
- Checks in with each sponsor throughout day
- Handles requests and questions
- Takes photos of sponsor activities

### Immediately After the Event

**Send a recap email within 48 hours:**

Include:
- Thank you message
- Event statistics (attendance, projects submitted, etc.)
- Photos of sponsor's involvement
- Social media metrics (reach, engagement)
- Participant feedback about sponsor
- Preliminary results/impact

**Purpose:** Keep the relationship warm while the event is fresh

### Long-Term Relationship Building

**6-12 months after the event:**

**Rekindle the relationship for next year:**
- Send update on implemented projects
- Share long-term impact metrics
- Preview plans for Year 2
- Early invitation to sponsor again

**MLH Recommendation:** A good way to maintain company relations is by keeping in touch to give sponsors a chance to commit to supporting your event again the next year.

## 📊 Measuring Sponsor ROI

### Metrics to Track and Share

**For Recruiting-Focused Sponsors:**
- Number of resume submissions
- Number of recruiter conversations
- Number of interviews conducted
- Post-event applications received

**For Brand Awareness Sponsors:**
- Website impressions (sponsor logo views)
- Social media reach (posts mentioning sponsor)
- Event attendance (brand exposure to N people for X hours)
- Photos/videos featuring sponsor brand
- Media mentions in press coverage

**For Developer Relations Sponsors:**
- Number of projects using their API/platform
- Quality of feedback received
- GitHub repos created with their tech
- Developer sign-ups to their platform

**For All Sponsors:**
- Participant satisfaction score with sponsor
- Quality of interactions reported
- Photos of engagement
- Post-event survey results

### Post-Event Sponsor Report Template

Send 2-3 weeks after event:

**Subject:** Hack for RVA Impact Report - Thank You [Company]!

**Include:**
1. Event overview (attendance, projects, outcomes)
2. Sponsor-specific metrics (their ROI data)
3. Photos featuring their brand/representatives
4. Participant testimonials about their involvement
5. Winning project spotlight (especially if used their tech)
6. Implementation updates (civic impact)
7. Media coverage links
8. Thank you and invitation to Year 2
