# Sponsor Outreach Process

**Based on MLH's 5-Step Sponsorship Process**

Landing quality sponsorships is manageable if you **break the process into small steps** and give yourself plenty of lead time. The more practice you get approaching and negotiating with potential backers, the better you'll become at the art of fundraising.

---

## 🎯 Core Principle

**Sponsors are like investors — they want to see you're serious before they give you capital.**

In the same way you wouldn't approach for venture capital without a product prototype, don't reach out to sponsors until you've got evidence that your hackathon is well on its way to happening.

**Evidence of legitimacy:**
- ✅ Confirmed date and time (March 6-8, 2026)
- ✅ Confirmed venue (CoStar HQ)
- ✅ City partnership (Mayor's Office MOU)
- ✅ Event website live
- ✅ Pre-registrations from participants
- ✅ Professional prospectus

### Step 2: Get In Touch

#### 🚨 CRITICAL EMAIL RULE

**Your first email should NEVER contain:**
- ❌ Links or hyperlinks
- ❌ Attachments
- ❌ Images (including signature images)
- ❌ Meeting invite links
- ❌ Sponsorship prospectus
- ❌ Website URL (in first email)

**Why?** Many companies have IT policies that automatically trash external emails with these elements to prevent phishing.

**Include prospectus/links in follow-up message 1-2 weeks after initial email!**

#### Crafting Your First Email

**What to Include:**

| Element | Why | Richmond Example |
|---------|-----|------------------|
| **Event details** (size, location, dates) | Essential first info sponsors seek | "Hack for RVA will take place March 6-8, 2026 at CoStar's Richmond HQ. We expect 150-200 participants." |
| **Overview of what a hackathon is** | Audience might not be familiar | "A hackathon is a weekend-long innovation marathon where Richmond's technologists, students, and community members collaborate to build solutions for our city's challenges." |
| **Engaging angle for sponsor** | Connect to their interests | "I see Dominion Energy's focus on sustainability and innovation — we're excited to feature climate resilience as a key challenge aligned with RVAgreen 2050." |
| **What makes your event unique** | Differentiate from other hackathons | "As Richmond's first civic innovation hackathon, we're partnering directly with Mayor Avula's office to ensure winning projects can actually be implemented by the City." |
| **Request to chat** | Phone calls convert better | "I'd love to discuss how [Company] and Hack for RVA can partner together. Do you have availability for a brief call next Tuesday at 2 PM EST?" |
| **Eye-catching subject line** | People get tons of emails | "Hack for RVA + [Company] Partnership Opportunity" or "Help Solve Richmond's Biggest Challenges This March" |

#### Who to Contact

**Reach out to the relevant decision maker.** When in doubt, email the CEO.

**Useful job titles (in priority order):**

1. **Developer Relations / Dev Rel / Developer Advocate**
   - Goal: Get real-time feedback on their products (APIs, dev tools)
   - Budget: Yes, typically have dedicated hackathon budget
   - Familiar with hackathons: Yes
   - Note: More niche/harder to find role

2. **Recruiting / Talent Acquisition / HR**
   - Goal: Recruit interns and full-time employees
   - Budget: Yes, recruiting budget
   - Familiar with hackathons: Often need explanation
   - Best for: CarMax, Capital One, large employers

3. **Marketing / Public Relations**
   - Goal: Raise brand awareness or change perception
   - Budget: Yes, marketing budget
   - Familiar with hackathons: Need education + photos from similar events
   - Best for: Dominion Energy, Markel, corporate brand sponsors

4. **Community Relations / Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)**
   - Goal: Community investment, social impact
   - Budget: Yes, CSR budget
   - Familiar with hackathons: Often need education
   - Best for: Civic-focused sponsors

5. **Employee Resource Groups (ERGs)**
   - Goal: Support specific demographics (Women in Tech, LGBTQ+, etc.)
   - Budget: Sometimes, depends on company
   - Best for: D&I-focused partnerships

⚠️ **Do NOT reach out to:**
- Random Software Developers/Engineers
- General Managers (unless VP-level with budget authority)
- Entry-level employees

**If you have alumni connections:** Ask them to connect you to the job titles above to get budget approval!

#### Follow-Up Cadence

**Don't worry if you don't receive a response!** Your contact may have lost your email or had a busy week.

**Follow-up timeline:**
- **Initial email:** Send (no links/attachments)
- **+1-2 weeks:** Follow-up #1 (now include prospectus, website link)
- **+3-4 weeks (or 3-4 days if close to event):** Follow-up #2
- **+5-6 weeks:** Follow-up #3 (final attempt)

**Ping up to 3 times total.** After that, move on.

### Step 3: Hop on an Exploratory Phone Call

#### Before the Call

**Prepare your elevator pitch** based on your prospectus content. Sponsors haven't received your prospectus yet (per email rule), so you need to pitch verbally.

**Richmond Civic Hackathon Elevator Pitch Template:**

> "Thanks for taking the time to chat! So, Hack for RVA is Richmond's first civic innovation hackathon happening March 6-8 at CoStar's headquarters. We're bringing together 150-200 technologists, students, and community members for a weekend focused on solving real problems from Mayor Avula's Action Plan.
>
> What makes us different from a typical hackathon is our implementation pathway — the Mayor's Office has committed to piloting winning projects, and we have a 6-month incubation program with mentorship and micro-grants to help teams actually deploy their solutions.
>
> We're working with CoStar as our venue partner, and we're looking for [2-3 more anchor sponsors / companies like yours] to help us make this event amazing and ensure these civic solutions get built."

#### During the Call

**Your Goals:**
1. Help sponsor understand what a hackathon is (use analogies like "science fair for tech")
2. Sell your team and story — what makes Hack for RVA special?
3. Get them excited about civic impact
4. **Learn their goals** (let them do most of the talking!)
5. Understand their budget and success metrics
6. Schedule follow-up call

**Key Questions to Ask:**

1. **"Has [Company] sponsored hackathons or similar events before?"**
   - If yes: "What did you get out of it? What worked well?"
   - If no: "What types of community events does [Company] typically support?"

2. **"What are [Company's] key goals for community partnerships?"**
   - Listen for: recruiting, brand awareness, product feedback, CSR impact

3. **"What does success look like for you in a sponsorship?"**
   - Tailor your proposal to their specific metrics

4. **"Given the times you've sponsored events similar to this one, what have you spent and what did you get out of it?"**
   - Don't be afraid to ask about budget directly!
   - This helps you know which tier to propose

5. **"Are there specific challenges or themes [Company] is interested in supporting?"**
   - Align with: Climate, Jobs & Transit, Housing, etc.

**Let Them Talk!**
- Prompt with guiding questions
- Make the call conversational
- Learn their goals while sharing your expertise
- Build trust and credibility

**Before You Hang Up:**
- [ ] Confirm their interest level
- [ ] Schedule follow-up call (specific date/time)
- [ ] Ask if it's okay to send prospectus and website link via email
- [ ] Thank them for their time

### Step 4: Make A Proposal

#### Timing: IMMEDIATELY After Your Call

**Option A: Pre-Made Package** (Easiest)
Send your prospectus and highlight the tier most relevant to what they discussed:

> "Thanks again for the great conversation! Based on what you shared about [Company's] interest in [recruiting/brand awareness/etc.], I think our **Gold Tier ($7,500-9,000)** would be a perfect fit. This includes:
> - Branded challenge track/prize category
> - On-site recruiting space
> - Tech talk slot (30 min)
> - 5 mentor volunteer spots
> - [Full benefits listed]
> 
> I've attached our sponsorship prospectus with all the details. Would you be open to a follow-up call next week to discuss next steps?"

**Option B: Custom Proposal** (For strategic partners)
Put together a tailored package based on their specific needs:

> "Based on our conversation, I've put together a custom partnership proposal for [Company]. Given your focus on [their goal], we're proposing:
> - [Custom benefit 1]
> - [Custom benefit 2]  
> - [Custom benefit 3]
> Investment: $[amount]
> 
> This aligns with your goal of [their stated objective] while giving [Company] [specific value proposition]. I'd love to walk through this proposal on a call next week — do you have availability on Tuesday or Thursday?"

#### Key Elements

**Always include:**
- Clear sponsorship amount
- Complete list of benefits
- Why this is a good fit for the sponsor (reference your call)
- **Your sponsorship deadline** (e.g., "We're finalizing sponsors by December 15, 2025")
- Next steps / call to action

**Richmond-Specific Angles:**

For **Dominion Energy**: "Powering Richmond's Innovation" - literal + metaphorical alignment  
For **CarMax/Capital One**: Talent pipeline, access to 150-200 tech-minded participants  
For **PropTech companies**: CoStar partnership, PropTech for Public Good track  
For **Community-focused companies**: Direct civic impact, Mayor's implementation commitment

### Step 5: Follow Up And Track Your Progress

#### Follow-Up Timeline

**After sending proposal:**
- **+1 week:** Check-in email ("Do you have any questions about the proposal?")
- **+2 weeks:** Follow-up call or email
- **If approaching deadline:** More frequent follow-ups (every 3-4 days)

#### Track Everything

**Use a CRM system to track:**
- Contact name, title, company
- Email address, phone number
- Date of first contact
- Follow-up dates and responses
- Current status (interested, proposal sent, committed, declined)
- Sponsorship tier committed to
- Payment status
- Notes from calls

**Tools for Tracking:**
- [ ] **Airtable** (recommended for Richmond team)
- [ ] **Notion** database
- [ ] **Excel/Google Sheets** (minimum viable)
- [ ] **HubSpot CRM** (if budget allows)
- [ ] **Boomerang** for email tracking

#### When a Sponsor Says YES

**Immediate Actions:**

1. **Invoice them immediately**
   - Use professional invoice template
   - Include payment details (account, method accepted)
   - Include sponsorship tier and benefits
   - Payment due date (ideally 30 days before event)

2. **Create and send contract**
   - Finalize funding amount
   - Method of payment (check, wire transfer, ACH)
   - Benefits included
   - Cancellation terms
   - Both parties sign

3. **Add to sponsor tracking sheet**
   - Mark as "Committed"
   - Track invoice sent date
   - Track payment received date
   - Assign sponsor benefits coordinator

4. **Send welcome packet**
   - Thank you message
   - Timeline of sponsor benefits
   - Upcoming deadlines (workshop proposals, swag delivery, etc.)
   - Point of contact for questions

5. **Begin benefit delivery planning**
   - Logo on website
   - Social media announcement
   - Coordinate workshop/challenge details
   - Plan on-site presence

## 🎭 Understanding Your Sponsors

### Five Categories of Sponsors

**1. Financial/Monetary Sponsors** (Most common)
- Companies that pay for services like logo on website
- **Richmond targets:** Most companies (CarMax, Capital One, Markel, etc.)

**2. Presenting Sponsors** (Premium)
- Companies that co-host or co-brand your event
- ⚠️ Be careful not to give up too much control
- **Richmond example:** CoStar Group (venue + operations partner)

**3. In-Kind Sponsors**
- Companies that donate services or goods for free
- **Richmond examples:**
  - AWS, Azure, GCP (cloud credits)
  - Vercel, Railway (hosting platforms)
  - GitHub (developer tools)
  - Lovable.dev (no-code platform)
  - Local caterers (food donations)

**4. Strategic/Media Partners**
- Companies that help with marketing and promotion
- **Richmond targets:**
  - Richmond Times-Dispatch
  - WTVR (local TV)
  - Richmond BizSense
  - RVA Magazine

**5. Community Partners**
- Help recruit hackers, mentors, judges, volunteers
- **Richmond partners:**
  - RVA Tech
  - Code for VA
  - VCU Computer Science
  - Women Who Code RVA

**Note:** Companies can fit multiple categories! (e.g., Capital One could be Financial + Recruiting + Community Partner)

### ⚠️ Evaluating In-Kind Sponsorships

**We often see companies attempting to give away free credits or swag to avoid paying a sponsorship fee.**

**MLH Recommendation:** Steering clear of these or evaluate case-by-case

**Questions to ask:**
1. Does this actually provide value to participants?
2. Would we have to pay for this otherwise?
3. Is this company trying to avoid a cash sponsorship?
4. What's the real dollar value of this in-kind contribution?

**Richmond Approach:**
- Cloud provider credits: ✅ High value, we'd pay otherwise
- Random startup API credits: ⚠️ Evaluate carefully
- Swag with no cash: ❌ Decline politely

## 📊 Sponsors' Top Practical Concerns

### 1. Attendee Value

**Question:** "Are your hackathon's attendees a good fit for our company?"

**How to address:**
- Share demographic breakdown (students vs. professionals, skill levels)
- Highlight unique bridge: "We connect you with Richmond's civic-minded tech community"
- Emphasize quality over quantity: problem-solvers who care about their city

**Richmond stats to share:**
- 150-200 participants target
- Mix: 40% VCU/UR/VUU students, 40% professionals, 20% community
- Target: 40% female/non-binary
- Skills: Developers, designers, data scientists, policy experts, community organizers

### 2. Who Else Is Sponsoring?

**Question:** "What other companies are involved?"

**How to address:**
- Share confirmed sponsors (once you have them)
- Name-drop strategic partners: "We're partnering with CoStar Group and the Mayor's Office"
- For first sponsor: "We're looking for 2-3 anchor partners to join CoStar in supporting this inaugural event"

**Some companies want competitors** (competitive recruiting)  
**Others want to be the biggest name** (exclusive positioning)

Ask: "Do you prefer to be the only [industry] sponsor, or are you comfortable with competitors participating?"

### 3. Package Value

**Question:** "Are we getting our money's worth?"

**How to address:**
- Break down value per benefit
- Compare to other marketing/recruiting channels
- Quantify reach and impressions
- Show ROI from similar events

**Richmond value calculation example (Gold Tier - $7,500):**
- Event website traffic: 5,000+ visits ($1.50 CPM = $7.50 value)
- Social media reach: 20,000+ impressions ($2,000 value)
- Resume access: 150 resumes (recruiting event = $5,000+ value)
- On-site brand presence: 10 hours with 150 people ($3,000 value)
- **Total value: $10,000+ for $7,500 investment**

### 4. The Force (Gut Feel)

**Question:** "Does this team seem competent and professional?"

**How to address:**
- Be organized in all communications
- Respond promptly to emails
- Have clear answers to questions
- Provide professional materials
- Show evidence of planning (website, date, venue confirmed)

**Professionalism checklist:**
- [ ] Professional email signature
- [ ] Quick response times (<24 hours)
- [ ] Well-designed prospectus
- [ ] Clear, concrete event details
- [ ] Confident, prepared phone calls
- [ ] Organized follow-up process
