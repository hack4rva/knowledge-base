# The Complete Writing Framework: AWS Principles + Creative Bar-Raising

Amazon's writing culture is built on clarity, data, and directness. But the best writing doesn't just inform—it persuades, moves, and sticks. This framework combines both: the discipline of AWS principles with the craft of creative bar-raising.

**Use this for:** Technical documents, narratives, blog posts, persuasive writing, and any communication where both clarity and impact matter.

---

## Part 1: AWS Writing Principles (The Foundation)

These are non-negotiable. Master these first.

### 1. Be Direct

✅ **Use short sentences** (often under 30 words)
- Long sentences hide weak thinking
- Break complex ideas into clear steps
- One idea per sentence when possible

✅ **Avoid wordy phrases**
- ❌ "due to the fact that" → ✅ "because"
- ❌ "in order to" → ✅ "to"
- ❌ "at this point in time" → ✅ "now"

✅ **Eliminate "weasel words"** (vague adjectives/adverbs)
- ❌ "very important" → ✅ State why it matters
- ❌ "significantly improved" → ✅ "improved by 40%"
- ❌ "highly scalable" → ✅ "scales to 10,000 requests/second"

### 2. Use Data / Be Objective

✅ **Replace adjectives with numbers**
- ❌ "Sales increased significantly"
- ✅ "Unit sales increased by 40%"

✅ **Avoid subjective, unquantified statements**
- ❌ "The system is fast"
- ✅ "The system responds in under 200ms"

✅ **Quantify claims whenever possible**
- Every "better" should have a "by how much?"
- Every "many" should have an actual count
- Every "significant" should have proof

### 3. Avoid Jargon and Acronyms

✅ **Explain technical terms the first time they appear**
- Don't assume everyone knows your domain
- Define before you use

✅ **Use everyday language**
- Write so non-experts can understand
- Technical depth doesn't require technical obscurity

### 4. Use the "So What?" Test

✅ **Every piece of writing should answer:**
- Why does this matter?
- What should the reader do?
- What changes because of this?

✅ **The central idea should be clearly developed**
- Remove fluff
- Remove tangents
- Remove anything that doesn't serve the main point

### 5. Writing Drives Thinking

✅ **Writing forces clarity of thought**
- At Amazon, meetings start with silent reading of detailed narratives
- The process of writing forces you to understand and defend the idea, not just pitch it

✅ **Why these principles matter:**
- Writing clearly is not just about communication—it's about thinking
- Simplicity and clarity prevent misunderstandings in large, fast-moving organizations
- Quantification and "so what?" ensure writing is useful for decision-making, not just narrative or fluff

## Part 2: Creative Bar-Raising (The Craft)

Once you have clarity, add impact. These principles make writing memorable.

### 1. Clarity Is Baseline — But Voice Wins

✅ Sentences are clean, unambiguous, and free of filler
✅ The rhythm feels deliberate — not robotic
✅ Technical terms are explained through story or analogy rather than dictionary definitions
✅ You can read it aloud and it sounds human, not templated

**Test:** Could a smart outsider follow this and stay awake?

### 2. Lead With a Hook — Earn the Attention

✅ The first line poses a tension, question, or image that makes you want the next one
✅ Avoid "throat clearing" ("In today's fast-paced world…")
✅ Every paragraph either raises a question or answers one
✅ Openings should make the reader lean forward, not nod politely

**Test:** Does the opening make you care?

### 3. Anchor Every Idea in Something Concrete

✅ Abstract concepts ("innovation," "scalability") are paired with vivid, grounded examples
✅ Use metaphor sparingly but effectively — it should clarify, not decorate
✅ If a reader can't see or feel what you mean, it isn't clear enough

**Test:** Can you sketch it on a whiteboard?

### 4. Write for Curiosity, Not Compliance

✅ Each section earns its next by provoking curiosity ("What happens if…?" "Why does this matter?")
✅ Technical writing can still have suspense — structure your argument like a discovery, not a dump
✅ Cut anything that answers a question the reader hasn't asked yet

**Test:** Does the reader ever feel like they're discovering something, not being lectured?

### 5. Respect the Reader's Intelligence

✅ Don't over-explain — use pacing, not padding
✅ Trust readers to connect ideas; guide them, don't drag them
✅ Replace buzzwords with proof, data, or story

**Test:** Could this be said in half the words without losing meaning?

### 6. Emotion Is a Data Point

✅ Where appropriate, highlight stakes, surprise, or human consequence
✅ Show not just what was built, but why it matters
✅ Avoid empty adjectives ("amazing," "transformative") — evoke feeling through result or contrast

**Test:** Does the reader feel something when the problem is solved?

### 7. End With Momentum

✅ Conclusions point forward — "so what now?" not "that's all, folks"
✅ The last sentence should echo the core idea or challenge the reader to act
✅ No fade-outs or generic wrap-ups

**Test:** Does the ending make you want to build, think, or share?

### 8. Mechanics of Bar-Raising

✅ One ruthless edit pass for brevity — every sentence earns its keep
✅ One pass for music — read it aloud for cadence and flow
✅ One pass for reader empathy — if you were them, what would you need next?
✅ One pass for originality — remove anything that sounds like a press release

**Test:** Would this make someone in another org stop and say, "That's great writing"?

## Part 3: The Unified Editing Process

Use this 4-pass method to apply both frameworks:

### Pass 1: AWS Clarity
- Cut to 30 words or less per sentence
- Replace adjectives with data
- Remove jargon
- Add "so what?" to every section

### Pass 2: Creative Voice
- Read aloud for rhythm
- Add a hook to the opening
- Make one abstract idea concrete with story/metaphor
- Check: does this sound human?

### Pass 3: Structural Tightening
- Cut 20% of the word count
- Ensure each paragraph earns the next
- Remove throat-clearing
- Make the ending point forward

### Pass 4: Reader Empathy
- Would a smart outsider follow this?
- Would they stay awake?
- Would they feel something?
- Would they act?

## Examples: Before & After

### Example 1: Technical Description

**Before:**
AWS Lambda is a service that lets you run code without provisioning or managing servers.

**After (AWS Principles):**
AWS Lambda runs your code without servers. No provisioning. No management.

**After (+ Bar-Raising):**
With AWS Lambda, your code runs when it's needed — and disappears when it's done. No servers. No waiting. Just results.

---

### Example 2: Impact Statement

**Before:**
Our new feature significantly improved customer satisfaction and made the experience better for users across the platform.

**After (AWS Principles):**
The feature increased customer satisfaction scores by 32% and reduced support tickets by 18%.

**After (+ Bar-Raising):**
The feature increased customer satisfaction by 32%. Support tickets dropped by 18%. Customers who used it once came back 3x more often. The data told us what users already felt: this wasn't just better—it was what they'd been waiting for.

---

### Example 3: Opening Hook

**Before:**
In today's fast-paced world, businesses need to innovate quickly to stay competitive. This document outlines our approach to innovation.

**After (AWS Principles):**
This document outlines our innovation process.

**After (+ Bar-Raising):**
Last quarter, we shipped three features that failed. This quarter, we changed how we build. Here's what we learned.

## The Complete Checklist

Before you ship any document, ask:

**AWS Foundation:**
- [ ] Sentences under 30 words?
- [ ] Adjectives replaced with data?
- [ ] Jargon explained or removed?
- [ ] "So what?" clearly answered?
- [ ] Wordy phrases cut?

**Creative Bar-Raising:**
- [ ] Opening hooks the reader?
- [ ] Voice sounds human when read aloud?
- [ ] At least one concrete example or story?
- [ ] Reader feels curiosity or emotion?
- [ ] Ending points forward?

**Final Polish:**
- [ ] Cut 20% of word count?
- [ ] Read aloud for rhythm?
- [ ] Checked for reader empathy?
- [ ] Removed anything that sounds like a press release?

## When to Use What

**Use AWS Principles alone for:**
- Internal docs
- Technical specs
- Decision memos
- Data-heavy reports

**Use both frameworks for:**
- Blog posts
- Persuasive narratives
- Customer-facing content
- Leadership communication
- Anything that needs to move people

**The rule:** Clarity is always required. Voice is earned after clarity is achieved.

---

## Key Insight

Amazon's philosophy: Writing clearly is thinking clearly. By forcing ideas into structured, data-driven narrative form, you must understand and defend the idea—not just pitch it.

The bar-raiser addition: Once your thinking is clear, make it memorable. Because clear thinking that no one reads is just noise.

**Master both. Ship great writing.**
