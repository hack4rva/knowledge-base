# Strategic Implementation Framework for Richmond's Civic Innovation Hackathon

**A Public-Private Partnership Between City of Richmond and CoStar Group**

---

## I. STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIP AND GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

### 1. Executive Synthesis: Maximizing Dual Stakeholder Value

The unprecedented institutional support from the City of Richmond (via the Mayor's Office) and CoStar Group (via venue and corporate commitment) necessitates a foundational shift in strategic focus. This event is no longer a viability test; it is an optimized mechanism designed to accelerate core strategic goals for both partners. The unified value proposition of the hackathon must be articulated as a targeted approach for delivering high-priority outcomes, leveraging the unique resources of the public-private partnership.

#### 1.1. Alignment with Richmond's Mayoral Action Plan (MAP)

The City's endorsement frames the hackathon as a critical component of Mayor Avula's policy agenda. The administration is guided by the Mayoral Action Plan (MAP), which organizes priorities around the **"Seven Pillars of a Thriving Richmond,"** including themes such as:

- Thriving City Government
- Thriving Neighborhoods
- Sustainable Built Environment

To secure sustained political backing, the hackathon must directly target specific, measurable outcomes tied to these pillars.

Analysis demonstrates that the City's primary metric for success extends beyond raw numbers to encompass demonstrable public trust, accountability, and effective service delivery. A traditional, general hackathon often yields non-implementable ideas, which dilutes public confidence. Conversely, by focusing challenges specifically on departmental pain points and mayoral priorities, the event transforms into a validated, cost-efficient civic procurement tool.

This approach ensures that the output is not merely a prototype but a potential solution to a known problem. Furthermore, the high visibility of the Mayor's participation allows the administration to demonstrate immediate action on core promises, translating the event into valuable political capital that justifies the ongoing public investment and secures support for subsequent years.

**Therefore, challenge selection must rigorously prioritize projects that promise quick public visibility and alignment with the MAP pillars.**

#### 1.2. CoStar Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and ROI Model

CoStar's commitment must be leveraged to demonstrate authentic community involvement that aligns with their corporate mandate, extending beyond merely supplying a venue. CoStar's corporate responsibility strategy emphasizes:

- Driving value for employees, shareholders, and communities
- Strong focus on sustainability (e.g., LEED and Energy Star certifications)
- Industry stewardship

Their core business involves expertise in real estate intelligence, GIS, and environmental data, including providing customers with green rating certifications and flood risk layers.

This domain expertise offers a substantial, non-generic contribution that elevates the partnership above standard corporate sponsorship. This specific PropTech knowledge aligns powerfully with the MAP pillar concerning **"A Thriving and Sustainable Built Environment"**.

The highest return on investment (ROI) for CoStar lies in establishing a specific **"PropTech for Public Good" track**. This dedicated focus:

- Validates CoStar's role as an industry steward
- Provides a sophisticated talent recruitment filter
- Offers a unique opportunity for CoStar employees to apply their specialized skills (GIS, data science) toward solving community challenges

The implementation plan must detail this track, linking CoStar's brand identity directly to Richmond's long-term sustainability goals.

### 2. Coalition Governance and Intellectual Property Strategy

A robust governance framework is essential to manage the inherent logistical and legal friction points between a municipal government and a large, publicly traded corporation, ensuring the longevity and legal clarity of the resulting projects.

#### 2.1. Partnership Governance and Decision Structure

The foundation of the partnership must be formalized through a **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** between the Mayor's Office and CoStar leadership, defining:

- Clear roles
- Resource commitments (cash and in-kind)
- Framework for Year 2 continuity

**Tri-partite Organizing Committee Structure:**

| Committee | Responsibilities |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Strategy & Policy (City)** | Challenge validation, securing government data access, political communications |
| **Operations & Logistics (CoStar)** | Venue security, facility access, catering coordination, internal employee volunteer management |
| **Community & Execution (External PM/Non-Profit)** | Participant recruitment, general marketing, vendor management, day-of-event execution |

This distribution of accountability, detailed via a **RACI matrix** (Roles, Responsibilities, Accountability, Consulted, and Informed), is critical for mitigating the operational friction often encountered in public-private ventures where approval processes or resource allocation can differ significantly between sectors.

#### 2.2. Intellectual Property (IP) and Implementation Licensing

Clarity on Intellectual Property (IP) ownership is the single most critical legal hurdle to successful City adoption of hackathon prototypes. While IP ownership generally defaults to the inventor, employment contracts and event terms can create complex conflicts.

To avoid prolonged legal and procurement battles over new technology adoption, the project must mandate a **"Civic Open Source Default" policy**.

**This policy requires:**

- All participants focused on a civic challenge intended for City adoption to register under terms that release the resulting prototype under a permissive Open Source license (e.g., MIT, BSD)
- CoStar must issue a pre-approved internal waiver for participating employees, confirming that any code contributed to a civic solution either uses public domain or permissively licensed intellectual property, or that CoStar irrevocably grants an Open Source license to the City for the resulting prototype

**Critical pre-event checkpoint:**

- The specific City department (likely IT or CAO's office) that will act as the long-term recipient and maintenance owner of the code must be identified
- City Legal counsel must review and approve the Open Source Software (OSS) policy template before the event launch

This proactive legal work transforms the hackathon into a legal/procurement fast-track rather than a mere idea generator, significantly improving the probability of implementation.

## II. EVENT FORMAT OPTIMIZATION AND PARTICIPANT EXPERIENCE DESIGN

This partnership necessitates a detailed operational plan that overcomes the unique challenges associated with hosting a community-wide civic event within a large, strictly controlled corporate headquarters on a typical workday.

### 3. The Friday Venue Optimization Manual: Leveraging CoStar's Campus

#### 3.1. Security, Logistics, and Accessibility Planning

The CoStar headquarters, expected to house over 3,000 employees at full build-out, offers premium facilities, including:

- Auditorium
- Conference space
- Publicly accessible amenities like green roof terraces and amphitheater

**Logistics must confirm:**

- Adequate capacity for the target 150-200 participants
- Robust Wi-Fi capable of handling numerous simultaneous devices
- Power cords and A/V equipment

**Critical attention to accessibility and security:**

- Confirm compliance with ADA standards, including accessible entrances and elevators
- Ensure availability of gender-neutral restrooms
- Strict planning for access control (unauthorized access is a primary concern at large corporate events)

Given the high profile of the event (mayoral involvement, corporate host, civic topic), there is potential for organized political protest, as has been seen at major tech conferences. Therefore, the security plan must be collaboratively orchestrated with CoStar's in-house team well in advance.

**Messaging must focus on:**

- Transparent access protocols and safety
- Avoiding overly aggressive measures that could alienate the community, such as invasive bag searches

Public transportation subsidies offered to CoStar employees should be mirrored or communicated to attendees to address parking and transit complexity.

#### 3.2. Optimal Friday Schedule: The 12:00 PM – 10:00 PM Extended Sprint

The weekday format presents a barrier to broad community participation. The optimal solution is the **12:00 PM – 10:00 PM Extended Sprint**. This format strategically addresses scheduling conflicts:

- The noon start allows working professionals to attend by taking only a half-day of Planned Time Off (PTO) or leveraging flexible work arrangements
- Students can attend after morning classes are complete
- The 10-hour duration provides sufficient time for viable prototype creation while avoiding the logistical complexity and prohibitive cost of a full 24-hour or overnight event

The initial two hours (12:00 PM – 2:00 PM) are crucial for inclusion and team cohesion. This time must be dedicated to:

- Shared communal breakfast or lunch
- Keynote/Welcome address (featuring the Mayor and CoStar CEO)
- Guided activities such as icebreakers and team formation workshops

Organizing activities like a "team canvas" ensures that newly formed "flash teams"—which are prevalent in corporate hackathons—quickly establish roles and communication standards before serious coding begins.

**Optimal Friday Schedule (Example):**

| Time | Duration | Activity | Stakeholder |
|------|----------|----------|-------------|
| 11:30 AM | 30 min | Registration & Security Check-In | Event Staff, CoStar Security |
| 12:00 PM | 30 min | Networking Lunch/Communal Meal | Participants, Mentors |
| 12:30 PM | 45 min | Welcome & Keynote (Mayor/CoStar CEO) | City/CoStar Leadership |
| 1:15 PM | 45 min | Icebreaker & Challenge Overview | Organizers, Civic Sponsors |
| 2:00 PM | 6 hours | Hacking Period 1: Build & Mentor Sessions | Teams, City/CoStar Mentors |
| 5:00 PM | 60 min | Dinner Break & Tech Talk/API Demo | Sponsors, Participants |
| 6:00 PM | 3 hours | Hacking Period 2: Refinement & Testing | Teams |
| 9:00 PM | 30 min | Submissions Deadline & Final Prep | Teams |
| 9:30 PM | 45 min | Final Presentations & Judging | Judges (Mayor/CoStar Reps) |
| 10:15 PM | 15 min | Awards Ceremony & Closing Remarks | City/CoStar Leadership |

#### 3.3. Creating an Inclusive and Welcoming Environment

Research indicates that professional corporate environments can create psychological barriers, leading to low participation from certain community groups and resulting in a homogeneous audience. The CoStar brand is associated with high-end corporate professionalism.

To attract diverse RVA residents, the event must be clearly communicated as a **community event hosted at the corporate campus**, rather than an exclusively corporate event.

**The environment must be proactively welcoming:**

- Co-brand event signage to feature local Richmond landmarks and community-centric designs alongside the City/CoStar logos
- Utilize the most accessible, public-facing parts of the campus, such as open areas or the amphitheater
- Establish a dedicated **Community Ambassador program**, recruiting volunteers from local organizations like Women Who Code and RVA Data Hackers

These ambassadors serve as:

- The initial welcome committee
- Guides to help participants navigate the facility
- A necessary buffer between community members and corporate security protocols

### 4. Demand Generation and Recruitment Blueprint

Achieving the ambitious first-year attendance target (150-200 participants) requires a targeted strategy that overcomes the inherent difficulty of the Friday format. Recruitment messaging must be segmented and optimized for distinct audience groups.

#### Target Audiences:

**Students (VCU, UR, VUU):**

- Messaging must pivot from typical collegiate hackathon appeals that emphasize large cash prizes
- Focus on the unique value proposition: the ability to build a portfolio project that has immediate civic implementation potential
- High-value networking access to City policymakers and CoStar technical professionals
- **Crucial barrier mitigation:** Engage university administration and professors directly to secure limited excused absences or extra credit for participants, minimizing the academic cost of Friday attendance

**Working Professionals (RVA Tech Community):**

- The appeal must center on civic fulfillment and immediate, tangible impact on Richmond
- Target RVAtech user groups (RVA Data Hackers, ProductTank Richmond, etc.) to minimize outreach scatter
- Position the 12 PM start time as a minimal disruption to the work week, requiring only a half-day PTO

**City Employees:**

- Position the event as a sanctioned professional development opportunity actively supported by the Mayor's Office
- Allow for participation during regular work hours
- Focus on internal recruitment—departmental liaisons should recruit employees to solve challenges related to their own daily workflow pain points

#### Diversity & Inclusion Strategy:

Diversity requires intentional effort. Outreach must include:

- Explicit partnerships with organizations like RVAtech's Women in Tech group
- Local civic tech groups (such as the Code for America Brigade, if active in Richmond)
- Non-profit community organizations focused on community impact

To eliminate financial barriers often associated with corporate venue events (e.g., parking, transit), the implementation plan must recommend a limited **Scholarship/Bursary program** offering transit vouchers or stipends.

## III. CIVIC CHALLENGE DEVELOPMENT AND IMPLEMENTATION PIPELINE

The core mission of this public-private partnership is the creation of implemented solutions. This necessitates a rigorous methodology for scoping challenges and defining the post-hackathon adoption pathway.

### 5. The Civic Challenge Design Methodology

#### 5.1. Scoping for 10-Hour Feasibility

Challenges must adhere to the **"Micro-Problem, Macro-Impact" Principle**, ensuring they are focused enough to be realistically addressed within the 10-hour event window.

**Guidelines:**

- Large system overhauls should be avoided
- Focus on specific user experience enhancements, data visualization tools, or prototyping solutions
- Example: "Develop a low-fidelity mock-up of a unified city permit tracking interface" rather than "Digitize all permitting records"

The design must recognize that not all valuable civic solutions are code-based. Civic challenges often benefit from:

- Data analysis
- User research
- Design thinking

Consequently, challenges must explicitly welcome non-technical participants (policy experts, designers, community organizers). Judging criteria must reward:

- Low-fidelity, user-validated prototypes
- Collaborative alignment achieved by the team
- Technical complexity

These factors are crucial for the long-term sustainability and usability of the final solution.

#### 5.2. Detailed Civic Challenge Framework

The core deliverable of the planning phase is a set of fully scoped civic challenges that align with the Mayor's priorities and leverage CoStar's expertise.

**MAP Pillar Alignment and Challenge Scoping Matrix (Example Challenges):**

| MAP Pillar | Core Problem/Goal | CoStar Expertise Leverage | Sample Challenge Topic | Implementation KPI (City) |
|------------|-------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Sustainable Built Environment | Improve tree canopy monitoring / Urban heat island effect mitigation | GIS mapping, Environmental data, PropTech/Real Estate data | **Tree Equity Scorecard:** Design a simplified public-facing GIS dashboard to track tree canopy density and identify critical planting zones based on socioeconomic and environmental factors | Pilot readiness score; City Parks Department adoption of dashboard |
| Thriving City Government | Inefficient public works reporting / fragmented service requests | UX/UI design, Workflow optimization, Data visualization | **One-Click RVA Reporting:** Prototype a streamlined mobile interface for citizens to report non-emergency infrastructure issues, with automated routing to the correct department | Reduction in internal service request misdirection rate (3 months post-pilot) |
| Thriving Economy | Lack of accessible business license/zoning information for new small businesses | Data liberation (Data Day model), Information architecture | **Richmond Business Navigator:** Structure and organize existing public data to create a simple, searchable directory of zoning restrictions and required business licenses for common ventures | Citizen satisfaction with new online resource (tracked via survey) |

### 6. Post-Hackathon Adoption Pathway

The true measure of success for this civic hackathon is the adoption and sustained implementation of winning projects. Implementation failure often stems from the lack of a clear pathway, where initial enthusiasm fades and projects fall into the innovation **"Valley of Death"** due to a lack of resources or institutional championship.

#### 6.1. Implementation Case Study Review and Lessons Learned

Case studies from US municipal civic technology implementations reveal that the simple creation of a tool does not guarantee improved government-citizen interaction or e-government evolution; institutional structures must be ready to receive the innovation.

**Successful civic projects require three key elements:**

1. An identified champion
2. Ongoing resources
3. A long-term plan for the outputs

To mitigate the "Valley of Death," the Implementation Playbook must mandate the formation of a **Post-Event Implementation Task Force**. This permanent body should consist of:

- A designated City Implementation Champion (usually a high-ranking employee from the relevant department)
- A CoStar technical mentor
- A representative from the Community Partner organization

This Task Force must be resource-bridged for a minimum of six months post-event to provide the necessary continuity and technical stabilization.

#### 6.2. City Adoption & Mentorship Pathway Manual

The Playbook details a four-step mandatory implementation pathway:

**Step 1: Immediate Triage (Week 1 Post-Event)**
- The City Legal team must review all winning solutions to confirm the status of the Open Source licensing status

**Step 2: Implementation Champion Assignment (Week 2)**
- An official City employee is designated as the primary owner, responsible for internal advocacy, budgetary review, and securing the project's long-term success

**Step 3: Mentorship Cohort (Months 1-3)**
- Leveraging its in-kind resource commitment, CoStar provides dedicated technical mentorship (e.g., two hours per week) to the winning volunteer team
- This mentorship stabilizes the prototype's code base and prepares it for pilot testing, utilizing CoStar employee engagement hours

**Step 4: Pilot Program Approval (Month 3)**
- The City must pre-commit to allocating a nominal micro-grant (recommended $1,000 to $5,000) for the most promising winning solution
- This funding supports initial pilot testing within a single department, providing the necessary bridge funding to formal procurement or scaling if the pilot proves successful

## IV. FINANCIAL STRATEGY AND OPERATIONAL EXECUTION

### 7. Complementary Sponsorship Strategy: Value-Add Resource Acquisition

The core partnership eliminates the two most significant costs: venue and institutional credibility. The supplementary sponsorship strategy must therefore focus exclusively on acquiring high-value resources and enhancing the participant experience without undermining the core City/CoStar brand narrative.

#### Sponsor Categorization and Targets:

**The Essential (Food & Beverage):**
- Sponsorship is critical for covering food costs for the 10-hour event, freeing up cash reserves for prizes and insurance
- Focus must be on local Richmond catering companies

**The Technical (APIs/Platforms):**
- This includes cloud providers (AWS, Microsoft, GitHub) or relevant local tech startups
- These sponsors provide tangible value to hackers through API access, cloud credits, and specialized technical challenges

**The Talent (Prizes/Swag):**
- Local organizations seeking talent benefit from sponsoring high-quality prizes (MacBook Pros, iPads, as seen in VTHacks)
- Enhances the event's prestige and serves as an efficient talent acquisition filter

The unique access provided by the mayoral endorsement is a premium asset that must be leveraged. Supplementary sponsorship benefits should emphasize access—such as:

- Private networking events with City civic leadership and CoStar tech leads
- Guaranteed participation in the final judging panel alongside the Mayor's Chief of Staff

This high-level visibility justifies premium sponsorship tiers even though fixed venue costs are zero.

### 8. Budget Modeling and Financial Controls

The Implementation Playbook must contain detailed financial models across three scenarios:

- **Conservative:** 100 participants
- **Moderate:** 150 participants
- **Ambitious:** 200 participants

Since core costs are covered, the models focus strictly on scalable line items:

- Food and beverage
- Prizes
- Insurance
- Marketing materials
- Necessary technology platforms (registration, judging)

**Cost Savings Quantification** must explicitly report the estimated in-kind value of CoStar's contribution, typically falling within the $7,000 to $10,000 range (venue rental, security, Wi-Fi, A/V, and volunteer labor). This quantification demonstrates fiscal efficiency to the City and validates CoStar's ROI.

Finally, the budget plan must mandate a **non-negotiable 10% contingency fund** (estimated at $800 - $1,500). This fund addresses unforeseen operational risks common in corporate facilities, such as:

- Required backup internet access
- Unexpected insurance liabilities
- Specialized equipment rental

### 9. Communications, Media, and Stakeholder Engagement Strategy

#### 9.1. Integrated Brand Messaging Strategy

The communications strategy must unite three core brand pillars:

1. **Civic Innovation** (policy impact, City-led)
2. **Technical Excellence** (CoStar's technical capacity)
3. **Community Accessibility** (RVA-wide involvement)

The City's communication channels must be utilized for official announcements and endorsements, ensuring guaranteed earned media coverage, which contributes directly to the City's visibility KPIs.

Given the high-profile nature of the partnership, a **proactive crisis communications plan** is necessary. Pre-approved talking points must be developed to address potential media scrutiny regarding:

- The use of city resources
- The alignment with a large corporation

The narrative must consistently frame the collaboration as an authentic, cost-efficient resource mobilization effort focused exclusively on solving defined community problems.

#### 9.2. Marketing Campaign Toolkit

The Implementation Playbook includes a comprehensive **Marketing Campaign Toolkit** featuring a **12-week content calendar**. This calendar precisely maps announcements—such as:

- The release of Civic Challenge details
- Mentor sign-up dates
- Official Mayoral statements

—to specific distribution channels, including:

- The City website
- CoStar internal communication platforms
- External RVAtech meetups and partner networks

This coordinated outreach ensures maximum penetration across the target demographics.

## V. IMPACT MEASUREMENT AND SUSTAINABILITY

Defining success across two distinct institutional stakeholders requires a dual-track Key Performance Indicator (KPI) framework that provides verifiable data for both the City (accountability) and CoStar (ROI).

### 10. Dual-Stakeholder Success Metrics Framework

**Dual-Stakeholder Success Metrics (KPIs):**

| Metric Category | City of Richmond KPIs (Civic Impact) | CoStar Group KPIs (Corporate ROI) |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Impact Efficacy** | Percentage of submitted projects deemed "Pilot-Ready" by Implementation Task Force | Number of CoStar employees participating as technical mentors/volunteers |
| **Community Engagement** | Diversity metrics: participant representation by gender, skill level, and zip code | CoStar employee satisfaction (NPS score) with volunteer experience |
| **Financial/Operational** | Cost-to-Solution Ratio (cost of hackathon per implemented solution) | Returning Sponsor Rate for complementary partners (renewal) |
| **Perception** | Percentage of residents satisfied with new city services resulting from hackathon | Earned Media Value (EMV) tracking positive brand association with civic outcomes |
| **Long-Term Adoption** | Number of winning solutions receiving micro-grant funding or moving to formal pilot program (6 months post-event) | Talent pipeline metrics (e.g., number of participants identified for internship/recruitment based on performance) |

#### 10.1. Civic Impact KPIs (City Focus)

Metrics for the City must focus on measurable public sector performance. This includes:

- Tracking diversity metrics to ensure the event serves the entire RVA community, not just existing tech students, demonstrating citizen engagement
- The ultimate measure of success for the City is the quality and implementability of the solutions, quantified by the percentage of projects that are deemed "pilot-ready"
- Subsequent tracking of public satisfaction with the resulting services

#### 10.2. Corporate ROI KPIs (CoStar Focus)

CoStar requires quantifiable returns on its investment of venue, employee time, and brand capital. KPIs for CoStar center on:

- **Brand enhancement:** tracking positive earned media value associated with the event
- **Internal employee engagement:** measuring satisfaction of volunteers, and tracking utilization of technical expertise
- **Talent pipeline development**

The commitment of complementary sponsors, measured by the returning sponsor rate, serves as an additional validation of the event's overall corporate value.

### 11. Year 2+ Sustainability and Growth Model

The long-term success of this hackathon hinges on institutionalizing the effort to ensure it survives changes in political administrations or corporate executive shifts.

#### 11.1. Institutionalizing the Effort

While CoStar provides the physical infrastructure and the City provides political legitimacy, neither entity is structurally optimized for long-term, community-driven event organization. Sustainability requires creating an organizational home outside the direct control of either core partner.

The recommendation is to establish the **Richmond Civic Innovation Coalition (RCIC)**. This non-profit or non-partisan community organization would assume responsibility for:

- Long-term project management
- Volunteer continuity
- Resource diversification

The RCIC should leverage existing community infrastructure, either by:

- Integrating with RVAtech's broader network
- Partnering with the Community Foundation's Civic Innovations team, which already works to foster local engagement

This external body can pursue independent grants and volunteer leadership pipelines, guaranteeing that the hackathon model remains robust regardless of changes within City Hall or CoStar.

#### 11.2. Knowledge Transfer and Replication

The comprehensive Implementation Playbook itself serves as the definitive knowledge transfer tool, documenting:

- All operational processes
- Vendor contracts
- Institutional lessons learned

For Year 2 and beyond, the strategy should emphasize **deepening impact** (e.g., implementing micro-grants for project continuation, increasing post-event support) rather than simply scaling attendance numbers.

Measurable implementation success, as defined by the Dual-Stakeholder Success Metrics, provides the best possible evidence base for securing continued institutional support and replication in subsequent years.

## VI. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The partnership between the City of Richmond and CoStar Group has fundamentally transformed the civic hackathon concept from a low-certainty experiment into a high-potential implementation vehicle. The core challenge is no longer feasibility but meticulous execution and strategic alignment.

### The analysis leads to the following expert conclusions and actionable recommendations:

**1. Mandate Partnership Governance and Legal Clarity**
- The project must proceed immediately with formalizing the MOU, establishing the tripartite organizing committee, and approving the Civic Open Source Default IP policy
- Pre-vetting the procurement pathway with City Legal and designating the code maintenance department is non-negotiable for ensuring implementation success

**2. Adopt the 12:00 PM – 10:00 PM Format**
- The research strongly supports the Friday Extended Sprint model
- The schedule must prioritize team formation and challenge scoping workshops (12:00 PM – 2:00 PM) to maximize output quality and integrate diverse participants

**3. Prioritize Implementable Challenges**
- The event must only pursue "Micro-Problem, Macro-Impact" challenges explicitly tied to the Mayor's Action Plan and scoped for 10-hour resolution
- A dedicated "PropTech for Public Good" track is recommended to maximize CoStar's ROI and civic value

**4. Institutionalize the Implementation Pathway**
- To overcome the "Valley of Death," the Playbook must mandate a dedicated, six-month Post-Event Implementation Task Force backed by a CoStar technical mentorship commitment and City micro-grant funding for pilot programs

**5. Establish Dual Accountability**
- Success measurement must be validated against both the City's civic impact goals (e.g., diversity, project implementation rate, service quality) and CoStar's corporate ROI goals (e.g., earned media, employee satisfaction, talent funnel)

**6. Create a Sustainability Vehicle**
- The long-term viability of the effort requires the establishment of an independent body, such as the Richmond Civic Innovation Coalition (RCIC), leveraged from existing local organizations (RVAtech, Community Foundation Civic Innovations team) to ensure continuity beyond the current leadership terms
