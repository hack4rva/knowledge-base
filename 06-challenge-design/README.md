# Challenge Design

Civic challenge creation, problem scoping, judging criteria, and implementation pathways for the Richmond Civic Hackathon.

---

## 🎯 Challenge Design Philosophy

### "Micro-Problem, Macro-Impact" Principle

Challenges must be:
- **Focused enough** to be realistically addressed within 10 hours
- **Specific enough** to generate implementable prototypes
- **Impactful enough** to create real civic value

**Avoid:** Large system overhauls
**Focus on:** Specific user experience enhancements, data visualization tools, prototypes

**Example:**
- ❌ "Digitize all city permitting records"
- ✅ "Develop a low-fidelity mock-up of a unified city permit tracking interface"

## 🏛️ Mayor's Action Plan (MAP) Pillars

### 1. Thriving City Government
**Focus:** Efficiency, accountability, service delivery

**Example Challenge:**
- **One-Click RVA Reporting:** Prototype a streamlined mobile interface for citizens to report non-emergency infrastructure issues, with automated routing to the correct department

### 2. Thriving Neighborhoods
**Focus:** Community engagement, safety, quality of life

**Example Challenge:**
- **Neighborhood Resource Mapper:** Create an interactive map showing community resources (parks, services, programs) by neighborhood

### 3. Sustainable Built Environment
**Focus:** Climate resilience, urban heat, tree canopy, environmental sustainability

**Example Challenge:**
- **Tree Equity Scorecard:** Design a simplified public-facing GIS dashboard to track tree canopy density and identify critical planting zones based on socioeconomic and environmental factors

### 4. Thriving Economy
**Focus:** Business development, job access, economic opportunity

**Example Challenge:**
- **Richmond Business Navigator:** Structure and organize existing public data to create a simple, searchable directory of zoning restrictions and required business licenses

## ✅ Challenge Selection Criteria

### Must Have:
- [ ] Aligns with Mayor's Action Plan pillars
- [ ] Scoped for 10-hour completion
- [ ] City department champion identified
- [ ] Data/resources available or obtainable
- [ ] Clear implementation pathway defined
- [ ] Success metrics established

### Should Have:
- [ ] Leverages CoStar PropTech expertise (for at least 1-2 challenges)
- [ ] Appeals to diverse skill sets (technical and non-technical)
- [ ] High public visibility potential
- [ ] Quick political win for Mayor's office

### Nice to Have:
- [ ] Multi-year evolution potential
- [ ] Replicable for other cities
- [ ] Research/academic interest

## 🏆 Judging Framework

### Scoring Categories (100 points total)

**Civic Impact (30 points)**
- Addresses real community need
- Potential for meaningful change
- Aligns with City priorities

**Feasibility & Implementation (25 points)**
- Realistic to implement
- Clear next steps defined
- City adoption pathway identified

**User-Centered Design (20 points)**
- User research conducted
- Intuitive and accessible
- Validated with target users

**Technical Quality (15 points)**
- Code quality and documentation
- Appropriate technology choices
- Scalability considerations

**Team Collaboration (10 points)**
- Diverse team composition
- Clear role distribution
- Effective teamwork demonstrated

## 🚀 Challenge Development Process

### Phase 1: Discovery (Month 12-11)
1. Meet with City departments
2. Review Mayor's Action Plan priorities
3. Identify pain points and opportunities
4. Gather available datasets

### Phase 2: Scoping (Month 10-9)
1. Draft challenge descriptions
2. Validate with City stakeholders
3. Assess 10-hour feasibility
4. Identify required resources

### Phase 3: Refinement (Month 8-7)
1. Get City Legal review
2. Confirm data access
3. Assign department champions
4. Define success metrics

### Phase 4: Launch (Month 6-5)
1. Publish challenges to participants
2. Host Q&A sessions
3. Provide starter datasets/resources
4. Connect teams with mentors
